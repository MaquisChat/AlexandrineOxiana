Code colours: please look

The twelve colours the app gives to qualitative codes, drawn the way the app draws them.

What this page is

In the app, every code has a colour, a shape and a name; the shape and name are there so that a code is never told apart by colour alone. This page draws all twelve on the light theme’s backgrounds and on the dark theme’s backgrounds, side by side, then as text chips, then in one small row like a crowded transcript margin. The colours, shapes and names are read straight from the app’s files; none is approximated.

How to respond. Reply in the chat with swatch ids, for example “C4 and C5 look the same to me in the dark theme”. Say which theme and which section.

The two demo panels are fixed. The light panels always use the app’s light theme and the dark panels its dark theme, whatever your device is set to. Only the page around them follows your device.

Source files. packages/ui/src/theme/tokens.css (colours and backgrounds), packages/ui/src/theme/codePalette.ts (names and rules), packages/ui/src/components/codeGlyphs.ts and CodeSwatch.tsx (shapes and drawing). Generated from commit b5b9762; regenerate with pnpm review-pages.

What to check

  • At small size (the last section), in both themes: can you tell every pair apart? Name any two that look the same to you.
  • Is any colour hard to read against its background, in either theme?
  • Do the shapes help you tell neighbours apart, especially two similar colours (for example C1 and C2, or C5 and C6)?
  • Does each colour keep its identity between light and dark (the red one is still “the red one”)?
  • If you have colour-blindness or know someone who has, which pairs are hard?

Known compromises

Stated in codePalette.ts and tokens.css:

  • The rule for every colour: at least 3:1 contrast against the theme’s page background (--bg) and card background (--surface); in the dark theme also against the chip background (--chip).
  • Light theme, chip background: relaxed. The 3:1 rule is not applied to --chip in the light theme. The file says no light palette reaching 3:1 there also kept the colours far enough apart under colour-blindness simulation. Light colours are asked to reach only 2.8:1 on the chip, and the shape is outlined to stay visible.
  • Any two colours must differ by at least 15 (CIEDE2000) with normal vision and 12 under simulated red-green and blue-yellow colour blindness.
  • The palette was fitted by a program, not chosen by eye, within colour-family limits per theme (the file’s own words).
  • From the 13th code on, colours and shapes repeat and a small number badge tells them apart. Only the first twelve are shown here.

Measured here from the actual values (WCAG contrast ratio): light theme below 3:1: C3 on chip (2.96); C4 on chip (2.88); C7 on chip (2.89); dark theme: nothing below 3:1. Full table at the bottom.
